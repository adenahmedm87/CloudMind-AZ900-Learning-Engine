YOUR WORKFLOW

How to use CloudMind effectively

Use each question as a mini lesson, not just a chance to earn a point.

①

Choose your mode

New to Azure? Start with Beginner practice. Choose Topic practice when you know which area is difficult. Save Mock exam for when you have studied all three exam domains.

②

Answer before checking

Read the whole question, identify what it asks, and select your own answer before reading the explanation. Avoid guessing from familiar words alone.

③

Rate your confidence honestly

Being wrong while very confident may reveal a misconception. Being right while guessing means the topic still needs review. Treat both as useful signals, not failure.

④

Explain it in your own words

After the explanation, say why the correct option fits and why one tempting alternative does not. If you cannot explain it simply, revisit the official learning material.

⑤

Review and repeat later

Use Smart Review and My Progress to find weak areas. Revisit them the next day and again a few days later rather than repeating the same questions immediately.

⑥

Practise under exam conditions

When ready, take a Mock exam without notes or interruptions. Afterwards, study every uncertain answer, not only the ones marked wrong.

02
LEVEL ONE

Beginner — build your foundations

Goal: understand the language of cloud computing before memorising service names.

01Understand before memorising

Study these concepts first

  • What cloud computing means and why organisations use it.
  • Public, private and hybrid cloud; consumption-based pricing; CapEx versus OpEx.
  • Shared responsibility and the difference between IaaS, PaaS and SaaS.
  • Core terms: scalability, elasticity, reliability, high availability and serverless.

Best way to learn

For every term, write a one-sentence definition and one real-world example. Compare similar concepts in a small table. Then use Beginner practice and explain every answer aloud.

Move on when: you can explain the three service models and cloud models without looking at notes, and can give an example of when each is useful.

03
LEVEL TWO

Intermediate — connect Azure services to use cases

Goal: recognise what each service does and why you would choose it in a scenario.

02Compare and apply

Study the Azure architecture and service domain

  • Regions, availability zones, resource groups, subscriptions and management groups.
  • Compute: virtual machines, containers, Azure Functions, App Service and Virtual Machine Scale Sets.
  • Networking: virtual networks, subnets, peering, DNS, VPN Gateway, ExpressRoute and endpoints.
  • Storage: Blob, Files and other storage options; tiers, redundancy and migration tools.
  • Identity and security: Microsoft Entra ID, MFA, SSO, Conditional Access, RBAC, Zero Trust and defense in depth.

Best way to learn

Make comparison cards: service → purpose → when to choose it → what it is not. Use Topic practice to focus on one category at a time. Draw simple diagrams for regions, subscriptions and networks.

Move on when: you can choose a suitable service for a short scenario and explain why the closest alternative is less suitable.

04
LEVEL THREE

Advanced — exam readiness and reasoning

“Advanced” here means advanced preparation for a fundamentals exam, not advanced Azure engineering.

03Reason under pressure

Finish the management and governance domain

  • Cost factors, Pricing Calculator, Cost Management and tags.
  • Azure Policy, resource locks, Microsoft Purview and governance.
  • Azure portal, Cloud Shell, CLI, PowerShell, Azure Arc, ARM and infrastructure as code.
  • Azure Advisor, Service Health, Azure Monitor, Log Analytics, alerts and Application Insights.

Best way to learn

Use mixed-topic practice, then take the Mock exam with a timer. For every missed or guessed question, label the reason: knowledge gap, confused services, missed wording, or careless reading. Review that cause before taking another mock.

Ready for exam review when: your results are consistently strong across all domains, you can explain your reasoning, and you have checked the latest official exam objectives. Practice scores are preparation signals, not a guarantee of passing.

A simple verification routine
  1. Find the concept in the official AZ-900 skills outline.
  2. Open the linked Microsoft Learn module or Azure documentation.
  3. Check the wording and the context: what the service does, when to use it, and what it does not do.
  4. Return to CloudMind and answer again from understanding, not memory of the option letter.

CloudMind is an independent learning tool. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by Microsoft. Microsoft documentation and exam objectives may change; always check the official page for the latest information.
